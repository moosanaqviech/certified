Infinite Powers · Lesson 1

Ask people what calculus is about, and you hear three puzzles: curved shapes, things in motion, and things that change.

Strogatz argues that one idea cracks all three. He calls it the Infinity Principle.

It works in two halves. Cut a hard thing into infinitely many simple pieces. Then add the pieces back up.

The cutting is a pretence. Real things are made of atoms, yet treating them as smooth is exactly what makes the method work.

Each half answers its own questions. Cutting finds slopes and speeds. Adding back finds areas and totals.
