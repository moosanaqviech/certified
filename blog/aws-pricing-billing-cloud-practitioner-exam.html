AWS Pricing and Billing Concepts for the Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) Exam

The AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) exam tests pricing and billing through Domain 4: Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%): the pricing models (On-Demand, Reserved, Spot, Savings Plans), the cost tools (AWS Budgets, Cost Explorer, the pricing calculator), consolidated billing through AWS Organizations, and the support plans.

Last updated August 2026.

Worth more than its 12%

Domain 4 is the smallest CLF-C02 domain at 12% of scored content, roughly six of the 50 scored questions. But pricing literacy earns marks beyond it: Domain 1 (Cloud Concepts, 24%) covers cloud economics, including the shift from capital expenditure to operating expenditure and the consumption-based model, and those questions lean on the same ideas. Between the two, how-AWS-charges thinking touches closer to a fifth of the exam.

It is also the fastest material on the paper to lock down. The models, tools, and plans below are a finite list with clean one-line jobs, which makes Domain 4 the classic "cheap marks" domain: an afternoon of focused study covers it end to end.

The four pricing models

CLF-C02 tests the models at a conceptual level: given a described workload, pick the model that fits. No math, no rate cards.

ModelCommitmentReach for it when
On-DemandNone, pay per useUnpredictable or short-lived workloads, or you are just starting out
Reserved1 or 3 years, a specific instance configurationSteady-state workloads you can predict far ahead
Savings Plans1 or 3 years, an amount of spend per hourSteady spend where you still want flexibility in what runs
SpotNone, capacity can be reclaimedFault-tolerant, interruption-tolerant work chasing the deepest discount

The distinction questions actually probe: Reserved commits to a configuration, Savings Plans commit to spend, and Spot's discount exists because AWS can take the capacity back. A workload described as "cannot tolerate interruption" eliminates Spot no matter how attractive the price sounds.

The cost tools, by the verb in the question

Every cost-tool question hides a verb, and each verb maps to exactly one tool:

  • Estimate before you build: the AWS Pricing Calculator. Model a planned architecture and get a cost estimate before anything is deployed.
  • Alert before you overspend: AWS Budgets. Set a spending threshold and get notified when actual or forecasted spend crosses it.
  • Analyze what you spent: Cost Explorer. Visualize and break down past and current spend by service, account, or time period.
  • One bill for many accounts: consolidated billing in AWS Organizations. Pay once for all member accounts, and pool usage so volume discounts apply across the organization.

Swapping Budgets and Cost Explorer is the classic distractor pattern: Budgets looks forward and alerts, Cost Explorer looks backward and explains.

The support plans

The exam guide puts AWS support plans and resources in Domain 4's scope. The pattern questions use: describe a need, ask for the cheapest plan that meets it.

  • Basic: included with every account: account and billing support, documentation, and service health tooling.
  • Developer: the entry paid tier, business-hours technical support suited to development and testing.
  • Business: 24/7 technical support by phone and chat, the usual floor for production workloads.
  • Enterprise On-Ramp and Enterprise: the top tiers for business-critical and mission-critical workloads, with faster response targets and, at Enterprise, a designated Technical Account Manager.

The give-away signals: "production workload" points at Business or above, and any mention of a Technical Account Manager points at Enterprise.

How to rehearse this domain

Because the depth is identify-the-right-thing, the highest-yield rehearsal is mapping scenario phrases to answers: "estimate the monthly cost of a planned workload" is the Pricing Calculator, "notify the team when spend exceeds $500" is Budgets, "which service drove last month's increase" is Cost Explorer, "single bill across 40 accounts" is consolidated billing, "commit for a discount but keep flexibility" is a Savings Plan, and "batch jobs that can be interrupted, at lowest cost" is Spot. If those six connections are automatic, Domain 4 is close to solved.

FAQ

What billing topics are on the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam?

Domain 4 of CLF-C02 covers the pricing models (On-Demand, Reserved, Spot, Savings Plans), the cost management tools (AWS Budgets, Cost Explorer, the AWS Pricing Calculator), consolidated billing through AWS Organizations, and the AWS support plans. It is 12% of scored content, roughly six of the 50 scored questions.

What is the difference between Reserved Instances and Savings Plans on the exam?

Both trade a 1 or 3 year commitment for a discount, but Reserved commits to a specific instance configuration while Savings Plans commit to an amount of spend per hour and stay flexible about what runs. Scenarios that stress flexibility point at Savings Plans; a fixed, predictable configuration points at Reserved.

What is the difference between AWS Budgets and Cost Explorer?

Budgets looks forward: you set a threshold and it alerts when actual or forecasted spend crosses it. Cost Explorer looks backward: it visualizes and breaks down what you have already spent. "Notify" or "alert" in a question points at Budgets; "analyze" or "identify what drove costs" points at Cost Explorer.

Do I need to memorize AWS prices for CLF-C02?

No. The exam tests pricing concepts, not rate cards: which model fits a described workload, which tool answers a cost question, and which support plan meets a need. No question asks for a dollar figure from memory.
