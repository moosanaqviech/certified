Is Delta Live Tables Still on the Databricks Data Engineer Associate Exam?

Yes, but not under that name. Delta Live Tables (DLT) is still tested on the Databricks Data Engineer Associate exam as Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines. The current May 2026 exam guide uses Lakeflow naming throughout, so the declarative pipeline engine you may have learned as DLT is the same concept with a new label, and the exam now calls out Lakeflow Jobs explicitly.

Last updated July 2026 · reflects the May 2026 exam version.

The short version

The concept stayed. The name changed. If you are studying from material that says "Delta Live Tables," you are learning the right engine under a retired label. Databricks renamed DLT to Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines in 2025 and rolled the current exam guide over to Lakeflow terminology. Nothing about streaming tables, materialized views, or data-quality expectations was removed, so the topics you would revise for "DLT" are still exactly what the exam tests.

What the May 2026 syllabus actually changed

The current guide is the May 2026 version of the Databricks Certified Data Engineer Associate exam. Compared with older outlines, three things stand out:

  • Lakeflow naming across the board. Ingestion, transformation, and orchestration are all framed under the Lakeflow platform rather than the old product names.
  • A clear Lakeflow Jobs focus. The guide expects you to monitor job health, read DAG-based task graphs to spot upstream blockers, and compare current run times against historical baselines using the run-history view.
  • CI/CD as first-class content. Deploying and promoting workloads is called out alongside ingestion and transformation, not treated as an afterthought.

You can see the shift in the guide's own recommended self-study courses, which now carry names like Data Ingestion with Lakeflow Connect, Deploy Workloads with Lakeflow Jobs, and Build Data Pipelines with Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines. That course list, published in the official exam guide, is the clearest signal that Lakeflow is the vocabulary the exam now speaks.

The current exam sections

The May 2026 guide organizes the exam around these areas:

  • Databricks Data Intelligence Platform: the workspace, lakehouse architecture, and platform capabilities.
  • Data ingestion and loading: Lakeflow Connect, Auto Loader, and COPY INTO patterns.
  • Data transformation and modelling: ETL with PySpark, plus building pipelines with Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines.
  • Working with Lakeflow Jobs and CI/CD: orchestration, scheduling, and promoting workloads.
  • Troubleshooting, monitoring, and optimization: job health, performance baselines, and cluster diagnostics.
  • Governance and security: Unity Catalog, grants, and fine-grained access control.

Databricks periodically revises the guide. Always confirm the current sections in the official exam guide before you sit the exam.

What "Delta Live Tables" looks like on the exam now

When a question tests the declarative pipeline engine, it will use Lakeflow language, but the mechanics are the ones you already know from DLT:

  • Streaming tables and materialized views as the pipeline's target objects.
  • Expectations, the data-quality constraints that let you warn, drop, or fail on records that violate a rule.
  • Declarative dependencies, where you define the tables you want and Databricks resolves the execution order and orchestration.

So the practical answer to "do I still need to learn DLT?" is: learn the behavior, attach the current name to it. If a practice question frames everything as DLT and Workflows, the ideas are right but the labels predate the rebrand.

How to study so stale labels do not trip you

The one real risk is vocabulary. A candidate who revised from a 2023 course can know the concepts cold and still hesitate when a question says "Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines" or "Lakeflow Jobs." Two habits fix that:

  • Read each current name once against its old name until the pair is automatic: DLT to Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines, Workflows to Lakeflow Jobs.
  • Practice on material written against the current guide, not archived outlines, so the names you see while studying match the names on exam day.

FAQ

Is Delta Live Tables still on the Databricks Data Engineer Associate exam?

Yes. The declarative pipeline engine once called Delta Live Tables is still tested, now under its current name, Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines. The concepts and behavior are unchanged; only the label on the exam is new.

Did the Databricks Data Engineer Associate syllabus change?

Yes. The current exam guide is the May 2026 version. It adopts Lakeflow naming across ingestion, pipelines, and jobs, and calls out Lakeflow Jobs monitoring and CI/CD explicitly.

Do I still need to know the name Delta Live Tables?

Focus on the current names. The exam uses Lakeflow terminology, so recognize Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines and Lakeflow Jobs, and treat older DLT and Workflows study material as the same concepts with retired labels.

What is Lakeflow Jobs on the exam?

Lakeflow Jobs is the current name for Databricks orchestration, formerly Workflows and Jobs. The May 2026 guide expects you to monitor job health, read DAG task graphs, and compare run times against historical baselines.

Are streaming tables and expectations still tested?

Yes. Streaming tables, materialized views, and expectations (data-quality constraints) are core to Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines and remain on the exam. The rename removed none of these concepts.
