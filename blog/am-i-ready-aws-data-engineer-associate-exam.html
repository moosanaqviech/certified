How to know when you are ready for the DEA-C01

The honest answer to "am I ready for the AWS Data Engineer exam" is not a feeling, it is a set of signals you can check. Here is what exam-ready looks like across the four DEA-C01 domains, the gaps that mean wait, and a free way to find out in about eight minutes.

Last updated July 2026.

Readiness is about the whole exam, not one strong domain

The AWS Certified Data Engineer Associate (DEA-C01) exam is 65 questions in 130 minutes (50 scored, 15 unscored) for $150 USD, and the passing score is 720 on a 100 to 1,000 scale. It uses a compensatory scoring model: you pass the exam overall, not each domain separately.

That single fact shapes what "ready" means. A very strong domain cannot fully rescue a very weak one, because everything rolls into one scaled total. So readiness is not "I know ingestion cold," it is "I score above the bar across all four domains, with no domain weak enough to drag the total under 720."

What exam-ready looks like, domain by domain

The exam is built on scenario questions that ask which service fits a constraint, so the readiness signal in each domain is a decision you can make cleanly, not a definition you can recite.

The gaps that mean wait

A few patterns reliably mean you are not there yet, even if you feel prepared:

  • You default to one service regardless of the scenario. Always reaching for EMR, or for Kinesis Data Streams, is a sign you are pattern-matching on names instead of constraints.
  • You can define a service but not choose between two. Knowing what Amazon Data Firehose is does not help if you cannot say when it beats Kinesis Data Streams.
  • One domain is consistently far behind. Under compensatory scoring, a single weak domain (usually Domain 4, security and governance) can still sink an otherwise strong total.

The tell: readiness is diagnostic, not a vibe. If you cannot point to which domain is your weakest and why, that is the first thing to find out.

A faster way to find out than guessing

Instead of estimating, take a diagnostic. The free DEA-C01 readiness quiz is 12 scenario questions across all four domains, untimed, with no signup. At the end it gives you a per-domain readout: how you scored in each domain against how much that domain weighs on the exam, and exactly which unit to study next. Each question targets one common mix-up (Kinesis Data Streams vs Amazon Data Firehose, Glue vs EMR, IAM vs Lake Formation), so a wrong answer names a specific gap rather than a vague low score.

It is a diagnostic, not a timed practice exam. The job is to route you to the right place in the free course, not to simulate exam-day pressure.

From your result to a plan

Turn the readout into a next step honestly:

  1. Scoring near the top across domains: you are exam-ready. Tighten your single weakest domain, then book the exam.
  2. Strong but with one or two soft domains: a focused week or two on the flagged domains should get you over the line.
  3. Gaps in the core: work the flagged units in order before drilling practice exams, so you are building on a foundation instead of memorizing.

The scaled passing score is 720 of 1,000, not a raw percentage, so treat practice results as a signal, not a guarantee: scoring consistently in the low-to-mid 70s percent and up, with no domain far behind, is a reasonable green light. FAQ

How do I know if I am ready for the AWS Data Engineer Associate exam?

You can choose the right service for a scenario across all four domains, not just define services, and no single domain is far behind the others. Because DEA-C01 uses compensatory scoring, overall readiness across domains matters more than one strong area. A per-domain readiness quiz is the fastest way to see where you actually stand.

What score means I am ready for DEA-C01?

The official passing score is 720 on a 100 to 1,000 scaled range, which is not a simple percentage of questions. On practice questions, scoring consistently in the low-to-mid 70s percent and above, with no domain trailing badly, is a reasonable readiness signal. Confirm current details in the official exam guide.

How long does it take to get ready for the DEA-C01?

It depends on your starting point. If a readiness check already puts you near the top across domains, you may be ready now. If you are close with one or two soft domains, a focused week or two usually closes the gap. If the core has holes, plan to work the weak units in order first.

Is a readiness quiz the same as a practice exam?

No. A practice exam simulates the timed test to ask "can you pass." A readiness quiz is a shorter, untimed diagnostic that asks "where do you stand and what should you study next," giving a per-domain readout that points you into the right part of the course.
