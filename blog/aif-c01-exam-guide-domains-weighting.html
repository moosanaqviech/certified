AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01): Exam Domains and Weights

The AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) exam splits its 50 scored questions across five content domains: Applications of Foundation Models at 28%, Fundamentals of Generative AI at 24%, Fundamentals of AI and ML at 20%, and both Guidelines for Responsible AI and Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions at 14%.

Last updated August 2026.

The weights below describe how the 50 scored questions are distributed. The exam serves 65 questions in total, but 15 are unscored trial questions that are not identified while you sit it.

Generative AI is the majority of this exam

The most useful thing the weights tell you: Domains 2 and 3 together are 52% of scored content, roughly 26 of the 50 questions. AIF-C01 is not a general AI exam with a generative AI chapter bolted on, it is a generative AI exam with an AI and ML foundation underneath.

That changes what preparation looks like. Foundation models, prompt engineering, the difference between fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation, and how to judge a model's output are not advanced extras, they are the bulk of the paper. Amazon Bedrock recurs across both domains as the service delivering these capabilities.

The two 14% domains are smaller, not optional

Responsible AI and Security, Compliance, and Governance are the lightest domains individually, but together they are 28% of scored content, about 14 questions. That is the same footprint as Applications of Foundation Models on its own.

They are also the cheapest marks available. The material is conceptual rather than technical (bias and fairness, transparency and explainability, data protection and access control for AI workloads), and the named AWS features attach cleanly to specific concerns: Guardrails for Amazon Bedrock to output safety, SageMaker Clarify to bias and explainability. A candidate fluent in generative AI who skipped these two domains is leaving roughly 14 questions on the table.

How to split your study time

  • About half on Domains 2 and 3. Match the weighting, and go beyond half if generative AI concepts are new to you.
  • Do not over-invest in Domain 1. At 20% it matters, but it is vocabulary and lifecycle basics most people absorb quickly. It is a prerequisite for the rest, not a destination.
  • Give Domains 4 and 5 one dedicated pass each. An afternoon apiece is usually enough, and skipping them costs about 14 questions.
  • Work from the exam guide's in-scope services list. Questions are written from the task statements, and the guide names the services that can appear in them.

Because scoring is compensatory, you are aiming at a single scaled total of 700, not five separate bars. In practice that means no domain has to be perfect, but none can be blank either.

FAQ

What are the domains on the AWS AI Practitioner exam?

AIF-C01 has five content domains: Fundamentals of AI and ML (20%), Fundamentals of Generative AI (24%), Applications of Foundation Models (28%), Guidelines for Responsible AI (14%), and Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions (14%). The percentages are shares of scored content, not fixed question counts.

Which AIF-C01 domain has the most questions?

Applications of Foundation Models is the largest at 28% of scored content, roughly 14 of the 50 scored questions. It covers prompt engineering, fine-tuning versus retrieval-augmented generation, and evaluating how a foundation model performs.

How much of the AIF-C01 exam is generative AI?

Just over half. Fundamentals of Generative AI (24%) and Applications of Foundation Models (28%) together make up 52% of scored content, about 26 of the 50 scored questions. Generative AI is the core of this exam, not a side topic.

Do I need to pass every AIF-C01 domain separately?

No. AWS uses a compensatory scoring model, so you are graded on one overall scaled score against the 700 passing mark rather than domain by domain. A strong domain can offset a weaker one, though a domain you skip entirely is hard to compensate for.
