Spark Developer Associate Exam Sections and Weights: What Each of the Seven Covers

The Databricks Certified Associate Developer for Apache Spark exam has seven sections with official weights: Architecture 20%, Spark SQL 20%, the DataFrame API 30%, Troubleshooting and Tuning 10%, Structured Streaming 10%, Spark Connect 5%, and the Pandas API on Spark 5%. On a 45-question exam that is roughly 9, 9, 13 or 14, 4 or 5, 4 or 5, 2 and 2 questions.

Last updated October 2026.

The seven sections at a glance

Thirty-two objectives on 45 questions means most objectives are asked about once and the heavier sections' objectives can come up twice. Certify's full-length mocks sample the sections as 9, 9, 13, 5, 4, 3 and 2, which adds up to exactly 45.

Section 1: Apache Spark Architecture and Components (20%)

Seven objectives, the most of any section, which makes each one worth roughly a question:

  • The advantages and challenges of implementing Spark.
  • The core components: cluster, driver, worker nodes and executors, CPU cores, memory.
  • The architecture: DataFrame and Dataset concepts, the SparkSession lifecycle, caching, storage levels, garbage collection.
  • The execution hierarchy: application, job, stage, task.
  • Partitioning, including shuffles and partitions.
  • Execution patterns: actions, transformations, lazy evaluation.
  • The Spark modules: Core, Spark SQL, DataFrames, Pandas API on Spark, Structured Streaming, MLlib.

Know the defaults cold: spark.sql.shuffle.partitions is 200 and cache() on a DataFrame uses MEMORY_AND_DISK. The course covers this in Unit 1 (Chapters 01 to 09), starting with why Spark.

Section 2: Using Spark SQL (20%)

Only four objectives carry the same 20% as Section 1, so each is worth about two questions:

  • Reading from and writing to common sources (JDBC, files) with Spark SQL, including overwriting and partitioning by column.
  • Running SQL directly on files (ORC, JSON, CSV, text, Delta) and the save modes for output.
  • Saving to persistent tables with sorting and partitioning to speed later reads.
  • Registering DataFrames as temporary views to query with SQL.

The save modes are a favorite: "append", "overwrite", "ignore" and "errorifexists", the default. Unit 2 (Chapters 10 to 15) covers it, including save modes as a trade-off lesson.

Section 3: Developing DataFrame/DataSet API Applications (30%)

The largest section and the most code-heavy. Ten objectives:

  • Manipulating columns, rows and structure: adding, dropping, splitting, renaming, filtering, exploding arrays.
  • Deduplication and validation.
  • Aggregations such as count, approximate count distinct, mean and summary.
  • Dates: Unix epoch to date string, extracting date components.
  • Combining DataFrames: inner, left, broadcast and cross joins, multiple keys, union and union all.
  • Writing, overwriting and reading DataFrames with schemas.
  • Sorting, iterating, printing schemas, converting to lists.
  • User-defined functions, with or without stateful operators, including state stores.
  • Broadcast variables and accumulators.
  • The purpose and implementation of broadcast joins.

Expect four PySpark snippets per question where three use a real method wrongly. Two points that come up: union matches by position and keeps duplicates, and spark.sql.autoBroadcastJoinThreshold defaults to 10 MB. See broadcast hash join versus sort-merge join. Unit 3 (Chapters 16 to 28) covers the section.

Section 4: Troubleshooting and Tuning (10%)

  • Performance tuning and cluster utilization: partitioning, repartitioning, coalescing, identifying data skew, reducing shuffles.
  • Adaptive Query Execution (AQE) and its benefits.
  • Logging and monitoring: driver and executor logs to diagnose out-of-memory errors and underutilization.

AQE has been on by default since Spark 3.2, so it is usually part of the answer. The classic pair here is repartition versus coalesce. Unit 4 is Chapters 29 to 32.

Section 5: Structured Streaming (10%)

  • The engine: programming model, micro-batch processing, exactly-once semantics, fault tolerance.
  • Creating and writing streaming DataFrames, including output modes and sinks.
  • Basic operations: selection, projection, windows, aggregation.
  • Streaming deduplication, with and without a watermark.

Output modes are the most testable single decision; see append, update and complete output modes. Unit 5 is Chapters 33 to 36.

Sections 6 and 7: Spark Connect and Pandas API on Spark (5% each)

Two objectives each, about two questions each. Section 6 asks for the features of Spark Connect and the deployment modes (client, cluster, local); Section 7 asks for the advantages of the Pandas API on Spark and how to create and invoke a Pandas UDF. With four objectives behind about four questions, these are the best marks per hour of study on the exam. Local, client and cluster deploy modes covers the deploy-mode objective; the course covers both sections in Units 6 and 7 (Chapters 37 to 40).

How to use the weights

Spend study time roughly in proportion: Sections 1 to 3 are 70% of the exam. But do not skip the small sections. Section 2 has only four objectives for 20% of the marks, and Sections 6 and 7 are narrow enough to master in a few sittings. For the format, cost and a preparation plan, start with the complete Spark Developer Associate guide.

FAQ

What is the biggest section on the Databricks Spark Developer Associate exam?

Developing Apache Spark DataFrame/DataSet API Applications is the biggest section at 30%, about 13 or 14 of the 45 scored questions. It has 10 objectives, from column manipulation and joins to UDFs and broadcast joins.

Are the Spark Developer Associate section weights official?

Yes. Databricks publishes the seven weights on the certification page: 20, 20, 30, 10, 10, 5 and 5 percent. The exam guide itself lists the sections and objectives without percentages.

How many questions come from each section of the Spark Developer Associate exam?

Databricks does not publish per-section counts, but the weights imply about 9, 9, 13 or 14, 4 or 5, 4 or 5, 2 and 2 questions out of 45. Unscored items may also appear and do not change that split.

How many objectives does the Spark Developer Associate exam guide list?

The official exam guide lists 32 objectives across seven sections: 7, 4, 10, 3, 4, 2 and 2. With 45 scored questions, most objectives are tested once and some twice.
