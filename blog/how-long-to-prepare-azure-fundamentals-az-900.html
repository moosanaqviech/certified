How Long Does It Take to Prepare for the Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) Exam?

Most people need 1 to 2 weeks of part-time study, roughly 10 to 15 hours, to pass the Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) exam. Anyone already working in Azure or Microsoft 365 can often be ready in a few days. With no IT background at all, budget 4 to 5 weeks.

Last updated August 2026.

Pick your timeline by starting point

2 to 4 days
You already use Azure. Or you hold AWS Cloud Practitioner: the cloud concepts transfer, only the Azure names are new.
1 to 2 wk
Some tech familiarity, little Azure. The typical case: developers, admins, analysts, and project managers.
4 to 5 wk
No IT background. Sales, finance, or a career change. The vocabulary itself is the work, not the difficulty.

These assume around 7 hours a week. AZ-900 is a fundamentals-level exam with no prerequisites, aimed at proving cloud fluency rather than engineering skill, which is why the typical timeline is measured in days and short weeks rather than months. It is also the rare certification with no renewal pressure: Microsoft Fundamentals certifications do not expire, so there is no reason to rush a date you are not ready for.

Level, prerequisites, and the no-expiry policy are from the Microsoft Certified: Azure Fundamentals page. Timelines are our own estimates.

Let the domain weights allocate the hours

The AZ-900 study guide publishes three skills domains with weight ranges. Applied to a 12-hour plan, they split like this:

Weight ranges from the official AZ-900 study guide. Domain-by-domain detail in the complete AZ-900 guide.

The surprise in that table is Domain 3. Most self-study plans treat management and governance as an afterthought, but at 30 to 35% it is nearly the size of the services domain. Cost tools (the pricing and TCO calculators, Microsoft Cost Management), governance features (Azure Policy, resource locks, tags), and the monitoring and deployment tools are close to a third of the exam.

A concrete 2-week plan (the typical case)

Days 1 to 3
Cloud concepts. The benefits of cloud (high availability, scalability, elasticity, reliability), capital versus operating expenditure, the consumption-based model, IaaS versus PaaS versus SaaS, and public versus private versus hybrid deployment. Short, and everything later assumes it.
Days 4 to 8
Architecture and services. Regions, region pairs, and availability zones, then the resource hierarchy: resources, resource groups, subscriptions, management groups. Follow with the service categories: compute (virtual machines, App Service, Azure Functions, containers), networking (virtual networks, VPN Gateway, ExpressRoute), storage (blob, disk, file, and tiers), and identity with Microsoft Entra ID.
Days 9 to 12
Management and governance. The pricing and total cost of ownership calculators, Microsoft Cost Management, Azure Policy, resource locks and tags, then the tooling: portal, Azure CLI, Cloud Shell, Azure Advisor, Azure Monitor. Service level agreements and the service lifecycle sit here too.
Days 13 to 14
Practice assessment, then book. Take Microsoft's free official AZ-900 practice assessment, re-read the task statements behind anything you miss, and repeat it until your score is comfortably stable. It matches the real exam's style, so it is the honest readiness check.

Thirty minutes in the portal beats an hour of notes. A free Azure account, one resource group, and one storage account makes the resource hierarchy concrete. Domain 2 is far easier to remember once you have clicked through it.

Plan against the clock, not a question count

Microsoft does not publish a fixed question count for AZ-900. Its published guidance is that most Microsoft certification exams contain 40 to 60 questions, and that the count varies between exams and over time. What is fixed is the time: 45 minutes of question time inside a 65-minute seat, which works out to roughly 45 to 65 seconds per question across that range.

For revision, that means drilling recognition speed matters more than stamina. AZ-900 questions reward knowing what a service is for in one sentence: if your practice-assessment misses cluster in one domain, the fix is re-reading that domain's task statements in the study guide, because questions are written directly from them.

The passing score is 700 on a 1 to 1,000 scale, and it is scaled rather than a percentage, with no per-domain bar to clear. There is no published pass rate to calibrate against, so treat a consistently strong practice-assessment score as the signal to book.

FAQ

How long does it take to prepare for the AZ-900 exam?

Most people need 1 to 2 weeks of part-time study, roughly 10 to 15 hours. If you already work in Azure or hold AWS Cloud Practitioner, a few days of targeted review is usually enough, and with no IT background, budget 4 to 5 weeks.

How many hours of study does AZ-900 need?

Around 10 to 15 hours for the typical candidate. Split them by the published weights: about 5 hours on Azure architecture and services (35 to 40%), 4 on management and governance (30 to 35%), and 3 on cloud concepts (25 to 30%).

Can I pass AZ-900 in a weekend?

If you already use Azure day to day, yes: the exam mostly formalises vocabulary you have. From a standing start, a weekend is possible for experienced tech professionals but risky, because the management and governance domain covers tools most people have never opened.

Is AZ-900 harder to prepare for than AWS Cloud Practitioner?

No, the effort is similar, and slightly lower in our estimates: 10 to 15 hours against 20 to 25 for CLF-C02. Both are fundamentals exams about recognising the right service or concept, and holding either one shortens preparation for the other considerably.

What is the best way to know I am ready for AZ-900?

Microsoft publishes a free official practice assessment for AZ-900 that matches the real exam's style. A consistently strong score on it, with no single domain lagging, is the most reliable booking signal, and that is our guidance rather than an official threshold.
