AWS Machine Learning Engineer Associate (MLA-C01): Exam Domains and Weights

The AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer Associate (MLA-C01) exam spreads its 50 scored questions across four content domains: Data Preparation for Machine Learning at 28%, ML Model Development at 26%, ML Solution Monitoring, Maintenance, and Security at 24%, and Deployment and Orchestration of ML Workflows at 22%.

The four domains, side by side

The striking thing about MLA-C01's weighting is how flat it is. The gap between the largest and smallest domain is six percentage points, roughly three questions. On the DEA-C01 data engineering exam that spread is sixteen points, which makes triage easy. Here it does not.

What each domain actually tests

AWS organizes every domain into task statements, and exam questions are written from those statements. Reading them is the fastest way to find your blind spots. Worth knowing before you sit down: unanswered questions are scored as incorrect and there is no penalty for guessing, so never leave one blank.

Domain 1: Data Preparation for Machine Learning (28%)

Three task statements: ingest and store data, transform data and perform feature engineering, and ensure data integrity and prepare data for modeling. In practice that means data formats (Parquet, JSON, CSV, ORC, Avro, RecordIO), AWS storage and data services such as Amazon S3, AWS Glue, Amazon EMR, and Amazon Athena, and the SageMaker data tooling including Data Wrangler and Feature Store. Bias detection, class imbalance, and labeling workflows live here too. If you come from a data engineering background, this is your cheapest domain.

Domain 2: ML Model Development (26%)

Choosing a modeling approach, training and refining models, and analyzing model performance. Model selection is broader than it first looks: alongside SageMaker built-in algorithms, the guide expects you to know when a managed AI service (Amazon Rekognition, Amazon Transcribe, Amazon Translate) or a foundation model through Amazon Bedrock or SageMaker JumpStart is the right answer instead of training your own. Training coverage runs to hyperparameter tuning (random search, Bayesian optimization), regularization such as dropout and weight decay, and methods to cut training time including early stopping and distributed training. Knowing why you would choose recall over precision for a fraud case, or AUC over accuracy on imbalanced data, is worth more here than knowing algorithm internals.

Domain 3: Deployment and Orchestration of ML Workflows (22%)

Selecting deployment infrastructure, scripting that infrastructure, and using automated orchestration for CI/CD. This is where the endpoint types get tested against requirements: the guide names serverless endpoints, real-time endpoints, asynchronous endpoints, and batch inference, so being able to match each to a latency, payload, and traffic profile is the core skill. Deployment targets beyond SageMaker endpoints are in scope too (Amazon ECS, Amazon EKS, Lambda), as is infrastructure as code with AWS CloudFormation or the AWS CDK, and orchestration with SageMaker Pipelines or Apache Airflow.

Domain 4: ML Solution Monitoring, Maintenance, and Security (24%)

Monitor model inference, monitor and optimize infrastructure and costs, and secure AWS resources. Drift detection with SageMaker Model Monitor and SageMaker Clarify, A/B testing in production, observability through Amazon CloudWatch and AWS X-Ray, AWS CloudTrail for logging and triggering retraining, instance right-sizing, and the security layer: IAM roles and policies, encryption with AWS KMS, and VPC configuration. At 24% this is the second-heaviest domain, and it is the one candidates most often underestimate.

How to use the weights in your prep

Because the weights are nearly flat, the usual "hit the big domain first" strategy does not apply. A better read of the numbers is to split the exam in two: model work (Domains 1 and 2) is 54%, and operational work (Domains 3 and 4) is 46%. That near-even split is the whole point of the credential. It is an ML engineering exam, so roughly half of it is about running models in production rather than building them.

That tells you where the risk sits based on where you are coming from. Data scientists tend to be strong on Domains 1 and 2 and lose points on deployment options, IAM, and monitoring. Data and platform engineers tend to have the reverse profile and need time on evaluation metrics and tuning. Whichever half is your weak one is where the marginal study hour pays.

One scheduling caveat: MLA-C02

AWS is refreshing this certification. Registration for the updated MLA-C02 exam opens on September 1, 2026, and the last day to take MLA-C01 in English is September 28, 2026. The domain weights on this page are MLA-C01's. If your test date falls after the switch, download the MLA-C02 exam guide and check its weighting table before you plan your study time, because domain weights are one of the things AWS commonly adjusts in a version update.

FAQ

What are the four domains on the MLA-C01 exam?

The four domains are Data Preparation for Machine Learning (28%), ML Model Development (26%), Deployment and Orchestration of ML Workflows (22%), and ML Solution Monitoring, Maintenance, and Security (24%). The percentages apply to the 50 scored questions.

Which MLA-C01 domain has the most questions?

Data Preparation for Machine Learning is the largest domain at 28% of scored content, roughly 14 of the 50 scored questions. ML Solution Monitoring, Maintenance, and Security is second at 24%.

Do I need to pass each domain separately on MLA-C01?

No. AWS uses a compensatory scoring model, so you pass on your overall scaled score of 720 or higher, not domain by domain. The section feedback in your score report is guidance only.

How many questions is the MLA-C01 exam and how long do I get?

The exam has 65 questions in total, 50 scored and 15 unscored, and the time limit is 130 minutes. The unscored questions are not identified during the exam and do not affect your result.

Are the MLA-C02 domain weights the same as MLA-C01?

Do not assume they are. AWS often adjusts domain weights in a version update, so if you are sitting MLA-C02 (registration opens September 1, 2026), check the weighting table in the MLA-C02 exam guide rather than reusing the MLA-C01 figures.
