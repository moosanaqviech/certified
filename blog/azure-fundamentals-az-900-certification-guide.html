Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900): The Complete Certification Guide

AZ-900 is Microsoft's entry point to Azure: a foundational exam with no prerequisites, three skills domains, and a passing score of 700. This guide covers the format, what is tested, who it is for, and how to prepare.

Last updated August 2026.

What AZ-900 actually is

AZ-900 is a fundamentals-level certification. It is designed for people who want to prove they understand cloud concepts and core Azure services without needing hands-on engineering experience. That makes it useful for sales, finance, project management, and students, as well as a first step for anyone heading toward a role-based Azure certification later.

Because it is a fundamentals exam, there are no prerequisites. You do not need another certification or any prior Azure work to sit it. Microsoft also does not expire fundamentals certifications, so once you pass, the credential does not need annual renewal the way the associate and expert role-based certifications do.

The three skills domains and their weights

The AZ-900 study guide organizes everything the exam can test into three domains, each with a published weight range. Knowing the proportions tells you where to spend your study time.

Describe cloud concepts (25 to 30%)

The vocabulary of cloud computing, independent of Azure specifics. This covers the benefits of cloud (high availability, scalability, elasticity, reliability), the difference between capital and operating expenditure, the consumption-based model, and the three main cloud service models: infrastructure as a service, platform as a service, and software as a service. It also covers cloud deployment models: public, private, and hybrid.

Describe Azure architecture and services (35 to 40%)

The largest domain, and the one that is genuinely Azure-specific. It covers the core architectural components (regions, region pairs, availability zones, resource groups, subscriptions, and management groups) and the main service categories: compute (virtual machines, App Service, Azure Functions, containers), networking (virtual networks, VPN Gateway, ExpressRoute), storage (blob, disk, file, and storage tiers), and identity (Microsoft Entra ID, formerly Azure Active Directory). This is where most of your study hours should go.

Describe Azure management and governance (30 to 35%)

How you manage cost, stay compliant, and keep resources organized. This covers cost management tools (the pricing calculator, the total cost of ownership calculator, and Microsoft Cost Management), governance features (Azure Policy, resource locks, and tags), and the tools you use to deploy and monitor resources (the Azure portal, Azure CLI, Azure Cloud Shell, Azure Advisor, and Azure Monitor). Service level agreements and service lifecycle concepts sit here too.

How to prepare

The most efficient path for a fundamentals exam is short and focused:

  • Read the official study guide first. It lists the exact task statements under each domain. Exam questions are written directly from those statements, so it doubles as a blind-spot checklist.
  • Work through Microsoft Learn's free AZ-900 learning paths. They map one to one onto the three domains and cost nothing.
  • Take the official practice assessment. Microsoft publishes a free practice assessment for AZ-900 that matches the real exam's style, which is the safest way to gauge readiness.
  • Get light hands-on time. A free Azure account and 30 minutes clicking through the portal makes the architecture domain far easier to remember than reading alone.

Most candidates with some general tech familiarity prepare in a few focused sessions rather than weeks. If you are completely new to cloud, budget more time for the cloud-concepts and architecture domains, since that is where the unfamiliar vocabulary lives.

Is it worth taking?

AZ-900 will not by itself qualify you for an Azure engineering role. What it does well is prove baseline fluency: it is a credible signal on a resume for non-engineering roles that touch the cloud, and it is a low-risk way to confirm you enjoy the material before committing to a role-based path like Azure Administrator (AZ-104) or a developer or data track. As a first cloud certification, the combination of no prerequisites, a modest fee, and a credential that does not expire makes it an easy yes for most people weighing it.

FAQ

What score do I need to pass AZ-900?

You need a score of 700 or higher on a scale that runs from 1 to 1,000. The scaled score is not a straight percentage, so 700 does not mean 70% of questions correct.

How much does the AZ-900 exam cost?

In the United States the exam is $99 USD. The price is set per country, so it varies by region. Check the official certification page for your local price before booking.

Do I need experience or another certification first?

No. AZ-900 is a fundamentals-level exam with no prerequisites. It is designed as a starting point, so no prior Azure experience or certification is required.

Does the AZ-900 certification expire?

No. Microsoft fundamentals certifications do not expire, unlike the role-based associate and expert certifications, which require annual renewal.

How many questions are on the exam?

Microsoft does not publish a per-exam question count for AZ-900. Its published guidance is that most Microsoft certification exams contain 40 to 60 questions, varying by exam and changing as exams are updated. Plan around the 45 minutes of question time rather than a target number of questions.
