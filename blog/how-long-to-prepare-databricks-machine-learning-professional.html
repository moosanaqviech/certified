How Long Does It Take to Prepare for the Databricks Machine Learning Professional Exam?

Most candidates need 7 to 9 weeks of part-time study to prepare for the Databricks Certified Machine Learning Professional exam, roughly 55 to 70 hours. Engineers already running production ML on Databricks often need 4 to 5 weeks. Without Machine Learning Associate-level knowledge, budget 12 to 14 weeks.

Last updated November 2026.

Why it takes longer than the Associate

The Professional exam is 59 scored questions in 120 minutes against 47 objectives, and Databricks recommends one year of hands-on experience, double the Associate's six months. The gap is not volume, it is the kind of knowledge. Most objectives describe something you only meet once a model is in production: a monitor that has to be configured, a test that has to run in the right environment, a rollout that has to shift traffic safely.

Reading about those is slow to stick. The estimates below assume you will build at least part of a pipeline while you study.

Question count, time limit and the one-year recommendation are from the Databricks Machine Learning Professional certification page and the exam guide PDF linked from it (30 Sep 2025 edition). The week and hour estimates are ours.

Pick your timeline by starting point

4 to 5 wk
Production ML on Databricks. You already own a training job, a registered model and a serving endpoint or batch scorer.
7 to 9 wk
Associate-level. You hold the ML Associate or work at that level: you train and track models but have not run the MLOps side.
12 to 14 wk
New to Databricks ML. Strong in ML elsewhere, or new to both. Cover the Associate material first.

These assume 7 to 8 hours a week. If you are in the third group, the ML Associate study plan is the honest first step, and the Associate vs Professional comparison explains why.

Where the hours should go

This guide publishes official section weights, so the split is not guesswork.

SectionOfficial weightOf a 60-hour plan
1. Model Development44%About 26 hours
2. MLOps44%About 26 hours
3. Model Deployment12%About 8 hours

Two warnings sit inside that table. First, half of the MLOps section's 20 objectives are about drift detection and monitoring, the area fewest candidates have touched in real work. Second, Model Deployment looks small, but 12% of 59 questions is about seven questions over only five objectives, so each objective there is worth more than anywhere else on the exam.

A concrete 8-week plan (the typical case)

Week 1
Spark ML at scale. When distributed training is justified, pipelines, transformers and estimators, CrossValidator vs TrainValidationSplit, evaluators, batch and streaming scoring.
Week 2
Scaling and tuning. pandas UDFs and the Pandas Function API for one model per group, distributed Optuna logged to MLflow, Ray Tune, and the scaling trade-offs (vertical vs horizontal, data vs model parallelism, Ray vs Spark).
Week 3
Advanced MLflow and Feature Store. Nested runs, custom PyFunc models, point-in-time lookups, scheduled feature pipelines, Online Feature Stores, streaming and on-demand features.
Week 4
Lifecycle, testing and environments. The deploy-code pipeline across dev, staging and prod, versions and aliases in Unity Catalog, unit vs integration tests and where each runs, Declarative Automation Bundles.
Week 5
Retraining and drift. Retraining triggers, selecting the top model, drift tests for numerical vs categorical features, profile types, and building a monitor.
Week 6
Monitoring in depth. Baseline vs consecutive drift, alert thresholds, performance trends from inference tables, custom metrics, slicing, endpoint health.
Week 7
Model deployment. Blue-green vs canary, served entities and traffic split, scaling a high-traffic endpoint, registering and deploying custom PyFunc models, querying endpoints.
Week 8
Full mocks. Three timed 59-question, 120-minute mocks with a day between each. Re-read the chapter behind every miss before the next one.

Compress or stretch: already running production ML? Fold Weeks 1 to 3 into one week of review and keep the monitoring weeks whole. Short on platform time? Add a week after Week 3 to build a small end-to-end pipeline in a workspace before starting MLOps.

Two things that move the estimate

Whether you have monitored a model

Monitoring is ten objectives. If you have set up a data profiling monitor (formerly Lakehouse Monitoring) and read its drift metrics table, Weeks 5 and 6 shrink to one. If you have not, they are the weeks least safe to cut.

Whether your team already works in Git with CI/CD

The lifecycle and testing objectives assume pull requests, staging tests and release branches. Candidates who work that way daily find Week 4 mostly a matter of vocabulary; notebook-only teams should give it the full week, starting with deploy code vs deploy models.

There is no published passing score to aim at. We use 70% on timed unit exams as a target and 85% on full mocks as the bar for booking, both our guidance rather than an official threshold.

FAQ

How long does it take to prepare for the Databricks Machine Learning Professional exam?

Most candidates need 7 to 9 weeks of part-time study, roughly 55 to 70 hours. Engineers already running production ML on Databricks often need 4 to 5 weeks, and those without Associate-level knowledge should budget 12 to 14 weeks.

How many hours should I study for the Databricks ML Professional exam?

Plan for 55 to 70 hours in the typical case, at 7 to 8 hours a week. Split them by the official weights: about 44% on Model Development, 44% on MLOps and 12% on Model Deployment.

Is the Databricks Machine Learning Professional exam hard?

It is harder than the Associate because the questions are production scenarios with several workable options. Databricks recommends one year of hands-on experience, and the monitoring, testing and deployment objectives are difficult to learn from reading alone.

What should I study first for the Databricks ML Professional exam?

Start with Section 1, Model Development, because the later sections build on it. Then give MLOps the most protected time, since half of its 20 objectives cover drift detection and monitoring.

Can I pass the Databricks ML Professional exam without the Associate?

Yes. There is no prerequisite, so the Associate is not required. Candidates who skip it should still be comfortable with its material first: AutoML, MLflow tracking, feature tables and basic model serving.
