How Long Does It Take to Prepare for the AWS Solutions Architect Associate Exam?

Most candidates need 6 to 8 weeks of part-time study, roughly 60 to 70 hours, to pass the AWS Solutions Architect Associate (SAA-C03) exam. If you already design systems on AWS, 3 to 4 weeks is realistic. If AWS is new to you, budget 10 to 12 weeks.

Last updated October 2026.

Pick your timeline by starting point

3 to 4 wk
You design on AWS. VPCs, IAM roles and Multi-AZ databases are already part of your week.
6 to 8 wk
You use AWS, but in one corner. A developer, data engineer or sysadmin who knows some services well and others not at all.
10 to 12 wk
AWS is new. Cloud vocabulary and architecture trade-offs at the same time.

These assume 8 to 9 hours a week and are our estimates, not AWS figures. AWS recommends at least one year of hands-on experience designing cloud solutions on AWS, but there are no prerequisites, so the timeline is really a measure of how much of that year you need to make up.

Where the hours go

SAA-C03 scores 50 of its 65 questions. Applying the official domain weights to a 64-hour plan gives a sensible first split:

DomainWeightOf a 64-hour plan
1. Design Secure Architectures30%About 19 hours
2. Design Resilient Architectures26%About 17 hours
3. Design High-Performing Architectures24%About 15 hours
4. Design Cost-Optimized Architectures20%About 13 hours

Weights from the official SAA-C03 exam guide. The domains and weighting post lists all 14 task statements.

Then move hours toward your gaps. Developers usually underestimate VPC networking. Operations engineers usually underestimate decoupling with queues and events. Almost everyone underestimates cost, because the right answer often depends on one charge, such as cross-AZ data transfer or a NAT gateway, that never shows up in day-to-day work.

A concrete 8-week plan (the typical case)

Week 1
Accounts and identity. Regions and Availability Zones, the shared responsibility model, the root user, IAM policies and how AWS evaluates them, roles and AWS STS, AWS Organizations and service control policies.
Week 2
Network security. Public and private subnets, route tables, NAT gateways, security groups vs network ACLs, VPC endpoints, AWS WAF and AWS Shield, VPN and Direct Connect.
Week 3
Data protection. AWS KMS keys and key policies, TLS with AWS Certificate Manager, AWS Secrets Manager, AWS Backup, and S3 Block Public Access, versioning and Object Lock.
Week 4
Loose coupling. Amazon SQS, Amazon SNS and Amazon EventBridge, AWS Lambda, containers on Amazon ECS or Amazon EKS with AWS Fargate, Amazon API Gateway and AWS Step Functions.
Week 5
High availability and DR. Multi-AZ vs read replicas, Aurora Global Database, Route 53 routing policies and health checks, and the four DR strategies against RPO and RTO.
Week 6
Performance. EBS volume types, S3 vs EFS vs EBS, EC2 Auto Scaling, choosing a database type, caching, load balancers, and streaming and data lake ingestion.
Week 7
Cost. S3 storage classes and lifecycle rules, Savings Plans, Reserved Instances and Spot, right-sizing, DynamoDB capacity modes, and data transfer and NAT costs.
Week 8
Full-length practice. Two timed 65-question sessions, a review of every miss by domain, and one last pass through the weakest task statements.

Build one small VPC by hand. Two public and two private subnets, a NAT gateway, a security group and a gateway endpoint for S3. An hour in the console makes a whole week of Domain 1 questions easier to picture.

Pacing on the day

The exam allows 130 minutes for 65 questions, exactly two minutes each. That is enough time, but SAA-C03 scenarios are long, so the risk is rereading. Read the last sentence first to find what is being asked, then read the scenario for the requirement that decides it. Flag anything over three minutes and come back to it.

The passing score is 720 on a 100 to 1,000 scale. Scoring is compensatory, so aim for one strong overall total rather than a pass in every domain. A cost domain at 60% can be carried; a security domain at 40% usually cannot, because it is 30% of the paper.

Should Cloud Practitioner come first?

Only if AWS is new to you. Cloud Practitioner covers the vocabulary (Regions, core services, pricing models) that SAA-C03 assumes, and it is a shorter exam. Taking it first moves you from the 10 to 12 week tier into the 6 to 8 week tier for the associate. If you already use AWS at work, skip it and put those weeks into SAA-C03 networking.

FAQ

How long does it take to prepare for the AWS Solutions Architect Associate exam?

Most candidates need 6 to 8 weeks of part-time study, roughly 60 to 70 hours. People who already design on AWS can be ready in 3 to 4 weeks, and those new to AWS should budget 10 to 12 weeks.

How many hours of study does SAA-C03 need?

Around 60 to 70 hours for the typical candidate at 8 to 9 hours a week. Put the most time into Design Secure Architectures (30%) and Design Resilient Architectures (26%), which together are over half of the scored questions.

Can I pass SAA-C03 in a month?

Yes, if you already design or operate workloads on AWS. Four focused weeks is enough to fill gaps and practise; without that experience, a month is usually too short for the networking and cost material.

What should I study first for the Solutions Architect Associate exam?

Start with identity and VPC networking. IAM roles, subnets, route tables and security groups appear in questions from every domain, so learning them first makes everything after easier.

Is SAA-C03 harder than Cloud Practitioner?

Yes. Cloud Practitioner tests what AWS services are; SAA-C03 tests which design to choose for a scenario, with longer questions and multiple-response items. It is an associate exam that assumes hands-on experience.
