Databricks Machine Learning Associate Exam: Cost, Format and Question Types

The Databricks Certified Machine Learning Associate exam is 48 scored questions in 90 minutes for a USD 200 registration fee, delivered online proctored. Questions are multiple choice or multiple selection, no test aides are allowed, there are no prerequisites, and the certification is valid for two years.

48 scored questions, and why the total you see may be higher

The number people most often get wrong is the question count. The current exam guide specifies 48 scored questions. Older third-party summaries still say 45, which came from an earlier edition, so treat any count you did not read in the guide itself with suspicion.

Separately, the exam may present unscored items. These are not identified while you are sitting the exam, they do not affect your score, and Databricks factors extra time into the limit to account for them. The practical consequence is simple: if you count more than 48 questions on exam day, nothing has gone wrong, and you should not spend the session trying to guess which ones are the pilots.

At 48 scored questions in 90 minutes you have a little under two minutes each. That is comfortable for recall-style questions and tight if you get stuck, so flag and move on rather than burning four minutes on one item.

The two question types

Every question is either multiple choice (one correct answer) or multiple selection (more than one correct answer, and the question tells you how many to pick). There are no drag-and-drop, ordering, or case-study formats, and there is no coding environment: you read code, you do not write it.

The practical consequence is that the exam rewards precise recognition rather than production. You will be asked which MLflow component does a particular job, which AutoML output to use for a given goal, or which line of a snippet is wrong. Spotting the correct call among four plausible ones is exactly the skill being tested.

No test aides are allowed: no notes, no documentation, no second screen. Since you have to recognize specific API surfaces from memory, work in the notebooks and then rehearse from memory rather than re-reading.

About the passing score

Databricks does not publish a cut score for this exam, and the exam guide does not state one. The widely repeated "you need 70%" figure is not sourced from Databricks. Cut scores are set through statistical analysis and adjusted as exams are revised, which is exactly why the number is not published.

The useful way to plan around this is to stop targeting a percentage and target coverage instead. The exam guide lists 48 objectives and the exam has 48 scored questions, so the working assumption of roughly one question per objective is a reasonable planning model. Any objective you cannot explain is a question you are likely to lose.

How to register

  1. Create a Databricks certification account on the Databricks training and certification site, or sign in with an existing one.
  2. Select the Machine Learning Associate exam from the certification catalog and confirm you are booking the current version.
  3. Schedule a slot. Delivery is online proctored, so availability is broad and you can often sit within a day or two.
  4. Pay the USD 200 fee. Local taxes may apply. Databricks occasionally issues vouchers tied to events and training, so it is worth checking before you pay full price.
  5. Run the system check for your webcam, microphone, and connection ahead of exam day, not on the morning of.
  6. Check in early. You will need government-issued photo ID, a clear desk, and a private room for the proctor's room scan.

What the 48 questions cover

The exam guide organizes its objectives into four sections. It does not publish a percentage weight per section, so any weighting you see quoted (including ours below) is derived, not official. Since the guide lists exactly 48 objectives against 48 scored questions, counting objectives per section is a defensible way to estimate the split:

  • Databricks Machine Learning, 18 objectives (about 38%): AutoML, Unity Catalog and feature store tables, MLflow tracking and model lifecycle, ML runtimes, MLOps practice.
  • Model Development, 15 objectives (about 31%): training, tuning, evaluating, and selecting models.
  • Data Processing, 9 objectives (about 19%): exploratory analysis, cleaning, feature engineering, and splits.
  • Model Deployment, 6 objectives (about 12%): batch and real-time serving patterns and moving a registered model toward production.

Databricks Machine Learning and Model Development are together around 69% of the exam, so the platform-specific material and the modeling material are where the hours belong. For the full section-by-section breakdown, see the complete ML Associate certification guide.

FAQ

How much does the Databricks Machine Learning Associate exam cost?

The registration fee is USD 200, plus any applicable taxes. A retake is charged the full fee again unless you have a voucher.

How many questions are on the Databricks ML Associate exam?

The current exam guide specifies 48 scored questions in 90 minutes. Unscored items may also appear, they are not identified during the exam, they do not affect your score, and extra time is factored in for them.

What is the passing score for the Databricks ML Associate exam?

Databricks does not publish a passing score for this exam, and the exam guide does not state one. Cut scores are set by statistical analysis and change as exams are revised, so the widely quoted 70% figure is not an official number.

What types of questions does the exam use?

Questions are multiple choice or multiple selection. There is no coding environment and no test aides are allowed, so you read and evaluate code rather than writing it.

Can I take the Databricks ML Associate exam from home?

Yes. The exam is delivered online proctored, so you can sit it from home or a private office provided you have a webcam, a microphone, a stable connection, photo ID, and a clear, private room.
