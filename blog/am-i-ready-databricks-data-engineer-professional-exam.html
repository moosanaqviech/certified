How to know when you are ready for the DE Professional

The honest answer to "am I ready for the Databricks Data Engineer Professional exam" is not a feeling, it is a set of signals you can check. This exam rewards reasoning through production scenarios, not recognizing the right tool, so readiness means depth across every area. Here is what exam-ready looks like, the gaps that mean wait, and a free way to find out.

Last updated July 2026.

Readiness is depth across the whole exam, not one strong area

The Databricks Data Engineer Professional exam is 59 multiple-choice questions in 120 minutes for $200 USD, proctored. Databricks does not officially publish a passing score; around 70% is the widely cited threshold, so treat any practice result as a signal and confirm current details when you register. For the full format, all ten domains, and how to prepare, see the complete Professional certification walkthrough.

What makes this exam harder than the Associate is not more facts, it is the style: questions describe production scenarios where several options look plausible and only one holds up under load, failure, or an audit. So readiness is not "I can name the Lakeflow objects," it is "I can reason to the right one across all ten exam sections, with no section weak enough to sink me."

What exam-ready looks like, by area

The ten official exam sections group into five readiness areas. For each, the signal that you are ready is a decision you can make under pressure, not a definition you can recite. The percentages are the summed official weights, so they show where the points actually are.

The gaps that mean wait

A few patterns reliably mean you are not there yet, even if you feel prepared:

  • Stale terminology and defaults. Still thinking in "DLT" instead of Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines, or reaching for Z-Order and partitioning as best practice, signals knowledge from an older exam version. Liquid Clustering is the current layout recommendation.
  • You can define a feature but not choose between two. Knowing what a materialized view is does not help if you cannot say when it beats a streaming table for an aggregate that restates history.
  • You hand-roll what the platform provides. Writing a manual MERGE where APPLY CHANGES handles out-of-order records, or rerunning a whole job where a repair run would do, is a sign you are pattern-matching, not reasoning.
  • One area is consistently far behind. Because the exam samples all ten sections, a single weak area (often Optimization or Security) can drag an otherwise strong result under the line.

The tell: readiness is diagnostic, not a vibe. If you cannot point to which of the five areas is your weakest and why, that is the first thing to find out.

A faster way to find out than guessing

Instead of estimating, take a diagnostic. The free DE Professional readiness quiz is 12 scenario questions across the five areas above, untimed, with no signup. At the end it gives you a per-area readout: how you scored in each area against how much it weighs on the exam, and exactly which units to study next. Each question targets one common mix-up (streaming table vs materialized view, Liquid Clustering vs Z-Order, Delta Sharing vs Lakehouse Federation), so a wrong answer names a specific gap rather than a vague low score.

It is a diagnostic, not a timed practice exam. The job is to route you to the right place in the free course, not to simulate exam-day pressure.

From your result to a plan

Turn the readout into a next step honestly. This is a harder exam over a 37-chapter course, so the timelines are longer than the Associate's:

  1. Scoring near the top across areas: you are exam-ready. Tighten your single weakest area, then book the exam.
  2. Strong but with one or two soft areas: a focused two to three weeks on the flagged areas should get you over the line.
  3. Gaps in the core: work the flagged units in order before drilling practice exams, so you are building depth instead of memorizing. The Databricks Data Engineer Professional course maps unit by unit onto those areas, with Unit 1 free.

Because Databricks does not publish an official passing score, treat practice results as a signal, not a guarantee: scoring consistently in the low-to-mid 70s percent and up, with no area far behind, is a reasonable green light. Always confirm current exam details when you register.

FAQ

How do I know if I am ready for the Databricks Data Engineer Professional exam?

You can reason through production scenarios and choose the right option across all five readiness areas, not just recognize features, and no single area is far behind the others. Because the exam samples all ten sections and rewards judgment under load, failure, and audit, depth across areas matters more than one strong spot. A per-area readiness quiz is the fastest way to see where you actually stand.

What is the passing score for the Databricks Data Engineer Professional exam?

How long does it take to get ready for the DE Professional exam?

It depends on your starting point, and most engineers need about three to six weeks. If a readiness check already puts you near the top across areas, you may be close; if one or two areas are soft, a focused two to three weeks usually closes the gap; if the core has holes, plan to work the weak units in order first.

Is the Professional exam much harder than the Associate?

Yes, mainly in style. Where the Associate often rewards recognizing the right tool, the Professional exam rewards reasoning through production scenarios where several options look plausible and only one holds up under load, failure, or an audit. That is why readiness here is about depth across areas, not memorized facts.
