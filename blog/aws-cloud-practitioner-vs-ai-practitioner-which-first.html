AWS Cloud Practitioner vs AWS AI Practitioner: which foundational cert first?

Take the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) first if you need AWS fluency or plan an AWS associate exam next; take the AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) first if your role is about AI and your next step is the Machine Learning Engineer Associate. Both are foundational, cost $100, and have no prerequisites, so neither is required before the other.

What each exam tests

On paper the two exams are twins: same tier, same fee, same question count, same clock, same passing score. The difference is entirely in the content, and it is large.

CLF-C02: the whole AWS Cloud, shallowly

Cloud Concepts (24%), Security and Compliance (30%), Cloud Technology and Services (34%), and Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%). The exam asks you to recognise the right service for a described need across compute, storage, databases, and networking, to split responsibilities between AWS and the customer under the shared responsibility model, and to know the pricing models, cost tools, and support plans. Machine learning services appear only at identify-the-category level inside the services domain.

AIF-C01: AI and generative AI, with AWS as the delivery vehicle

Fundamentals of AI and ML (20%), Fundamentals of Generative AI (24%), Applications of Foundation Models (28%), Guidelines for Responsible AI (14%), and Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions (14%). Just over half the scored content is generative AI: foundation models, prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation versus fine-tuning, and evaluating model output, with Amazon Bedrock as the recurring service. General AWS services barely feature outside the security domain.

AIF-C01 also uses the wider question-type list. Ordering and matching items award no partial credit, which makes it the slightly less forgiving paper of the two, even though both sit at the same level.

Where the two exams overlap

The overlap is real but narrow, and it sits in one place: AIF-C01's Domain 5 applies general AWS security to AI workloads. Data protection, identity and access control, and compliance resources are the same concepts CLF-C02 tests in its 30% security domain, so holding Cloud Practitioner turns that AIF-C01 domain into revision. The AI Practitioner study-plan post puts the saving at about a week.

Beyond that, the shared ground is format and framing: both are recognition exams written from task statements at describe-and-identify depth, both score compensatorily against one scaled total, and both assume the same consumption-based cloud economics. The service catalogue, the pricing models, and the support plans on CLF-C02 do not reappear on AIF-C01, and nothing about foundation models reappears on CLF-C02.

Which should you take first?

Cloud Practitioner first if...

You are new to AWS, your role touches the cloud broadly (technical, managerial, sales, purchasing, or finance), or your next certification is an AWS associate such as Solutions Architect, Data Engineer, or Machine Learning Engineer. CLF-C02 is the general grounding every AWS path assumes.

AI Practitioner first if...

Your work is about AI products, you already know AWS basics, or you are heading straight for the Machine Learning Engineer Associate (MLA-C02). AIF-C01 builds the generative AI vocabulary that runs through every MLA-C02 domain, and CLF-C02 would add little to that route.

Three signals decide it. Your role: general cloud fluency points at CLF-C02, AI fluency at AIF-C01. Your next exam: a cloud associate follows naturally from Cloud Practitioner, while the ML Engineer Associate follows from AI Practitioner. What you already know: if AWS identity, storage, and billing vocabulary is already familiar from work, skip CLF-C02 and go to AIF-C01; if both AWS and AI are new, Cloud Practitioner first is the gentler ramp, because AIF-C01 then asks you to learn two vocabularies at once.

Difficulty should not be the deciding factor. Both exams are foundational, and in the cluster's study-plan estimates they are within a week of each other: 2 to 3 weeks for CLF-C02 against 3 to 4 weeks for AIF-C01 from a typical starting point.

Taking both: the order that saves time

Many people take both, and the sequence matters more than it looks:

  1. Cloud Practitioner first. It supplies the AWS security, identity, and data-protection vocabulary that AIF-C01's Domain 5 reuses, and the general service names that AIF-C01 scenarios mention in passing.
  2. AI Practitioner second, about a week shorter. With CLF-C02 behind you, Domain 5 is revision, so the saved time goes into Applications of Foundation Models, the largest AIF-C01 domain at 28%.
  3. Then the associate that fits your role. Either exam is a ramp, not a requirement: AWS sets no prerequisites for its associate exams, so if you already build with SageMaker AI or Bedrock at work, both foundational exams are optional.

Reversed, the order still works, it just saves nothing: AIF-C01 teaches almost none of the CLF-C02 service catalogue, so Cloud Practitioner takes its full 2 to 3 weeks afterwards.

FAQ

Should I take AWS Cloud Practitioner or AI Practitioner first?

Take Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) first if you are new to AWS or your next exam is a cloud associate, and AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) first if your role is about AI or you are heading for the Machine Learning Engineer Associate. Both are foundational with no prerequisites, so neither is required before the other.

Is the AWS AI Practitioner harder than the Cloud Practitioner?

They are at the same level: both are foundational exams with 65 questions, 90 minutes, and a 700 passing score. AIF-C01 uses more question types, including ordering and matching items with no partial credit, and typical preparation runs about a week longer, but neither is meaningfully harder.

Do AWS Cloud Practitioner and AI Practitioner overlap?

Only in one area. AIF-C01's Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions domain (14%) applies the same identity, access, and data-protection concepts that CLF-C02 tests in its 30% security domain. The service catalogue, pricing models, and support plans on CLF-C02 do not appear on AIF-C01, and foundation models do not appear on CLF-C02.

Do I need Cloud Practitioner before the AI Practitioner exam?

No. AWS sets no prerequisites for either exam, so AIF-C01 can be your first AWS certification. Holding Cloud Practitioner first mainly makes the AI security and governance domain feel like revision and can shorten AIF-C01 preparation by about a week.

How much do the AWS Cloud Practitioner and AI Practitioner exams cost?

Both cost $100 USD, the AWS foundational-tier price, before any local tax added at checkout. Each certification is valid for three years, and retakes are paid in full.

Heading for the associate tier after either exam?

Certified has no Cloud Practitioner or AI Practitioner course yet. Its AWS courses start at the associate level, with full Data Engineer Associate (DEA-C01) and Machine Learning Engineer Associate (MLA-C02) courses, which is where both foundational paths lead.
