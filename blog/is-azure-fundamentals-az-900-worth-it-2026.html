Is Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) Worth It in 2026?

Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) is worth it in 2026 if your employer or target role runs on Azure and you need a credential proving baseline cloud fluency: students, non-engineers in Microsoft shops, and anyone starting an Azure path. It is a weaker use of time for engineers already working in Azure daily, who should go straight to a role-based exam.

Last updated October 2026.

What it actually costs you

AZ-900 is about as cheap as a vendor certification gets. The exam is $99 USD in the United States (Microsoft prices by country), you answer questions for 45 minutes inside a 65-minute seat, and you need 700 on the 1 to 1,000 scale. There are no prerequisites, Microsoft's learning paths and official practice assessment are free, and the credential does not expire, so there is no renewal fee or renewal assessment later. The cost and format post has the detail, including why Microsoft publishes no fixed question count.

The time cost is the smallest of any exam Certified writes about. The AZ-900 study plan puts the typical candidate at 1 to 2 weeks, roughly 10 to 15 hours; someone already working in Azure or Microsoft 365 can be ready in a few days, and someone with no IT background should budget 4 to 5 weeks. That is the trade to weigh: a very low cost against a credential that, by design, proves fluency rather than skill.

Who it is worth it for

Worth it if you are...

An employee of a Microsoft shop (Microsoft 365, Entra ID, Azure) in a sales, finance, project or support role who needs to follow cloud conversations; a student or career changer who wants a first credential that never needs renewing; or a developer, administrator or analyst at the start of an Azure path who wants the architecture and governance vocabulary before a role-based exam such as Azure Administrator (AZ-104).

Skip it if you are...

An engineer who already deploys to Azure every week, since the role-based exam is where the recognition is; someone whose employer or target roles build on AWS, where Cloud Practitioner is the matching credential; or someone expecting the badge alone to open an engineering role, which no fundamentals exam does.

What the certification does and does not do

  • It does prove you can describe cloud concepts, the core Azure services and how Azure is governed and billed. The three domains are Describe cloud concepts (25 to 30%), Describe Azure architecture and services (35 to 40%) and Describe Azure management and governance (30 to 35%), and questions are written from the study guide's task statements.
  • It does teach the part most self-study skips: management and governance is close to a third of the exam, covering the cost calculators, Azure Policy, resource locks and service level agreements. The pricing, SLA and governance post covers that domain on its own.
  • It does stay valid. Microsoft fundamentals certifications never expire, which is unusual and makes AZ-900 a credential you earn once.
  • It does not qualify you for an Azure engineering role by itself, and it does not test configuration, scripting or design. The core services post shows the describe-the-service depth it stops at.

The realistic take: for anyone in or heading toward a Microsoft shop who does not yet have the vocabulary, AZ-900 is an easy yes: $99, a couple of weeks, and no renewal ever. For anyone who already has the vocabulary, the same two weeks belong to AZ-104 or the matching role-based exam.

AZ-900 or AWS Cloud Practitioner?

Both are entry-level exams with no prerequisites and a 700 passing score, so the choice is the cloud, not the exam. AZ-900 is the shorter sitting at 45 minutes of question time against 90 for CLF-C02, and it never expires, where Cloud Practitioner is valid for three years. If nothing decides it for you, count which cloud the job listings you want name. Taking both is cheap, because cloud models, pricing and shared responsibility work the same way on each. FAQ

Is AZ-900 worth it for beginners with no IT background?

Yes, if Azure is the cloud you will work alongside. AZ-900 has no prerequisites, costs $99 USD in the United States, never expires, and tests cloud concepts and Azure services at describe-the-service depth. Without an IT background, budget 4 to 5 weeks rather than the typical 1 to 2, because the vocabulary itself is the work.

Does AZ-900 help you get a job?

On its own, no. AZ-900 proves baseline cloud fluency, which is a credible line on a resume for non-engineering roles that touch Azure, and a useful first step toward a role-based certification. For an Azure engineering role, employers look for a role-based credential such as AZ-104 and hands-on experience.

Should I take AZ-900 before AZ-104?

Only if Azure is new to you. Microsoft positions AZ-900 as a starting point rather than a requirement, and its value before AZ-104 is the architecture and governance vocabulary the role-based exam assumes. Treat it as a one-to-two-week ramp, not a required step.

Is AZ-900 worth it if I already have AWS Cloud Practitioner?

Only when Azure is entering your work. The cloud concepts transfer and the second exam is mostly new service names, so holders of Cloud Practitioner can usually be ready in 2 to 4 days. If your roles stay on AWS, your time is better spent on an AWS associate exam.

Does the AZ-900 certification expire?

No. Microsoft fundamentals certifications, including Azure Fundamentals, do not expire and need no annual renewal. That differs from Microsoft's role-based certifications, which require a yearly renewal assessment, and from AWS Cloud Practitioner, which is valid for three years.
